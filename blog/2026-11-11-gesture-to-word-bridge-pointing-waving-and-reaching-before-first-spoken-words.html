Why Pointing and "Showing" Are the Launchpad for Spoken Nouns

When your toddler holds up a half-eaten cracker to show you or points at a dog across the street, they are doing something huge: they are initiating a conversation! Even if no spoken word comes out yet, their brain is saying, "Hey Mama, look at this with me!" The moment you respond by naming what they pointed to ("Dog! Big brown dog!"), you plug the exact right word into their open attention window.

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Does teaching gestures or simple baby signs slow down spoken speech?

Never! Clinical studies prove the exact opposite: pairing a natural gesture (like waving, pointing, or tapping your lips for "eat") with the spoken word actually speeds up spoken word growth and reduces toddler frustration.

What if my toddler pulls my hand toward things instead of pointing with their finger?

When your child leads your hand to the pantry or a toy shelf, gently model pointing with your own index finger to touch two choices ("Touch cracker... or touch banana?") and invite their finger to tap the one they want!
