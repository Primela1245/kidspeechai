Why '-ING' Is the #1 Grammatical Milestone for 2-Year-Olds

In speech-language development (Brown's Stages of Language), the present-progressive -ING ending is the very first grammatical marker children acquire—even before plurals (-s) or past tense (-ed)! Why? Because toddlers live in the right-now present moment: they can watch someone jumping, drinking, or brushing right in front of their eyes.

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

At what age should a child start using '-ING' words like 'running' or 'eating'?

Most toddlers begin using their first '-ING' verbs (without 'is', such as 'Baby sleeping' or 'Me jumping') between 19 and 28 months, and add the helper verb ('Baby IS sleeping') between 28 and 36 months.

Why does my toddler answer 'Dog!' when I ask 'What is the dog doing?'

That means your toddler hears 'What...' and defaults to naming the object! Bridge it by validating the noun first and emphasizing the action: 'Yes, that's a dog! And look at his legs—the dog is RUNNING!'
