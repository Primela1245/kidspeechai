Core Words vs. Fringe Words for Toddlers: Why "Go, More, Open, Stop & Help" Beat ABC Flashcards

Published 2026-11-17 · Kid Speech AI Early Learning Team · 5 Min Read

✨ Quick Parent Takeaway

Speech-language educators divide early vocabulary into two types: 'Fringe Words' (specific nouns like 'bulldozer', 'strawberry', or 'hexagon' that only work in one situation) and 'Core Words' (universal power words like 'Go, Stop, More, Open, Help, Want, Up, Down, In, Out, Mine, All done' that work in EVERY room of the house!). What Is the Difference Between Core Words and Fringe Words?

Imagine your toddler learns the word "zebra." That is a fun Fringe Word—but unless you are at the zoo or looking at a safari book, your child cannot use "zebra" at breakfast, in the bath, or at the park. Now imagine your toddler learns the Core Word "Open!" They can say "Open door," "Open milk," "Open box," "Open book," and "Open car" 40 times every single day! Research shows that just 50 to 100 Core Words make up nearly 80% of everyday toddler conversation.

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Should we stop teaching nouns ('Fringe Words') like animals and vehicles?

Not at all! Toddlers love specific nouns (especially their favorite trucks, foods, and animals). The secret is pairing 1 Core Word + 1 Fringe Noun ('Go train!', 'Open banana!', 'Big dog!') so they can build two-word phrases!

Why are Core Words harder to show on static paper flashcards?

Because you can't draw a single picture of 'More' or 'All done'! That is why Kid Speech AI uses real-motion Pop-Up Video Flashcards, Live Action Narration, and real-world camera scanning to model Core Words in real life.

Which 3 Core Words should a late talker learn very first?

Start with 'More', 'Open', and 'Up' (or 'Help')—those three words give a toddler immediate power to request food, toys, and cuddles without crying!
