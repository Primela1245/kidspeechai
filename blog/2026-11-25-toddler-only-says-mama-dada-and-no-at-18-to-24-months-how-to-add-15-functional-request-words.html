Why Toddlers Get Stuck on 'Mama', 'Dada', and 'No'

Think about how powerful those three words are: saying "Mama!" or "Dada!" summons an adult who immediately guesses what the child wants, and saying "No!" stops anything they don't want. In other words, 3 words are currently doing the job of 50 words! To expand their vocabulary from 3 words to 20+ words, we select easy-to-pronounce bilabial (M, B, P) and alveolar (D, T, N) power words that give your toddler immediate control over their favorite toys and snacks.

5 Steps to Expand From 3 Words to 20+ Functional Request Words at Home

  1. 1. Build Upon Sounds They Already Can Pronounce (M, B, P, D): Because your toddler already says "Mama" (M) and "Dada" (D), their easiest next words use those exact same lip and tongue movements: "More", "Milk", "Ball", "Bubble / Pop", "Up", and "Down"!
  2. 2. Model Exclamatory 'Fun Words' First ('Uh-oh!', 'Boom!', 'Pop!', 'Whee!'): Exclamatory words use big musical pitch changes that are 3x easier for hesitant talkers to imitate than dry labels. Drop a block and say "Boom!" or "Uh-oh!" ten times in a row.
  3. 3. Use First-Person Toddler POV Action Videos for 10 Minutes a Day: Play the "Roll the Ball!", "Pop the Bubble!", and "Knock It Down!" POV video flashcards in Kid Speech AI so your toddler sees eye-level hands performing the action while hearing 27 cheerful voices.
  4. 4. Give Small Portions ('Sabotage Snack Time') to Spark 'More!' & 'Open!': Instead of pouring a full bowl of crackers, place 2 crackers on the tray and keep the rest in a clear closed container. Your toddler now has 10 natural chances to practice "More!" and "Open!" in one sitting.
  5. 5. Track Every New Sound Approximation on the First 100 Words Checklist: Celebrate "Ba" for ball, "Mo" for more, and "Uh" for up as official word wins!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

If my 18-month-old only says Mama, Dada, and No, can they catch up quickly?

Yes! Because they already have bilabial (M) and tongue-tip (D, N) consonants plus intentional communication, targeting 10 to 15 matching M/B/P/D request words during daily routines often sparks rapid word growth within weeks.

Should I count 'Ba' for ball if they already say 'Mama' clearly?

Absolutely. Single-syllable consonant-vowel approximations like 'Ba' (ball), 'Mo' (more), and 'Do' (dog) are completely normal at 18 to 24 months and count as real spoken words.
