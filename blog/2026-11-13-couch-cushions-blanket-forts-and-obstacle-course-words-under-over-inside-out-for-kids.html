Blanket Forts & Couch Cushion Obstacle Courses: "Under, Over, Inside, Crawl!" Word Games (Ages 2–7)

Published 2026-11-13 · Kid Speech AI Early Learning Team · 5 Min Read

✨ Quick Parent Takeaway

How do you get a 2- to 7-year-old to truly understand spatial words like "Under," "Over," "Through," "Inside," and "Behind"? Let them move their whole body through a living-room blanket fort and couch-cushion obstacle course! When a child crawls UNDER a blanket, steps OVER a pillow, and hides INSIDE a fort, their muscles and brain wire those prepositions and action verbs together permanently. Why Full-Body Movement Teaches Prepositions 3x Faster Than Worksheets

Pointing to a flat picture of a ball "under" a table is abstract for a toddler. Crawling on hands and knees under a kitchen chair draped with a bedsheet is unforgettable! Proprioceptive input (heavy muscle work like pushing heavy couch cushions, crawling through tunnels, and jumping onto pillows) wakes up your child’s attention system and makes spatial directions feel like a playground game.

3 Living-Room Fort & Obstacle Course Word Games

  1. The 3-Step Living Room Obstacle Course ("Over, Under, Jump!"): Line up two couch cushions on the rug and drape a blanket between two chairs. Narrate your child’s path like an excited sports announcer: "Step over the blue pillow... crawl under the blanket... and JUMP on the crash pad!"
  2. 3D AR Exercise & Action Narration Warm-Up Outside the Fort: Before crawling inside the fort, stand on the rug with 3D AR Exercise or Real-Time Action Narration in Kid Speech AI to do 60 seconds of jumping, waving, and clapping!
  3. The "Flashlight Fort Delivery" Scanning Game: Sit inside the blanket fort together with a flashlight. Send your child out on "missions" to bring one household object (a shoe, a spoon, a book, a ball) **inside** the fort and scan it for 2 seconds with Live Camera Object Naming!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

What if my toddler just wants to hide inside the blanket fort and play peekaboo?

Follow their lead! Lifting the blanket flap up and down 20 times in a row gives you 20 natural turns to practice "Open!", "Shut!", "Where's Maya?", "Peekaboo—inside!", and "Come out!"

How does heavy cushion lifting help hyperactive or sensory-seeking kids talk?

Pediatric occupational and speech therapists use "heavy work" (pushing, pulling, and carrying big cushions) because deep muscle pressure calms an overwhelmed nervous system so the child can focus and speak.

Can older kids (ages 4–7) be the "Obstacle Course Boss"?

Yes! Let your 4- to 7-year-old give YOU or a sibling the 3-step directions using sequence words: "First jump over the pillow, next crawl under the chair, last clap three times!"
