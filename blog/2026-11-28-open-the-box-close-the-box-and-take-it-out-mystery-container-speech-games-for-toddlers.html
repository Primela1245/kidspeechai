Why 'Container Temptation' Is a Speech Therapist's #1 Strategy for Late Talkers

When all of a toddler's toys are spread out on an open floor, they never need to talk to get what they want. When you place 5 favorite figurines or snacks inside a clear plastic jar or cardboard box with a snug lid, you create a playful communication temptation. Your toddler sees the toy they want and has an immediate, motivating reason to look at you and say "Open!"

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

What if my toddler gets frustrated when they can't open the container lid?

Never hold a container hostage until your toddler cries! Keep the lid loose, wait just 2 to 3 seconds after modeling 'Open!', and celebrate any eye contact, point, lip closure ('O!'), or word attempt by opening the box right away.

Why is 'Open' such an important first word for toddlers?

'Open' works across the entire house all day long—from opening snack wrappers, milk cups, and pantry doors to opening board books, toy boxes, and car doors.
