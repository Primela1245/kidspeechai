Why "Wh-" Questions Can Feel Tricky for Young Children

When a parent asks, "What did you do today?" or "Where is your shoe?", a young child has to process the question word, search their memory for the matching category (a person, an object, an action, or a place), and assemble the spoken words—all within a few seconds. If they don't know which category the question word points to, they often stay silent or repeat the last word of your question.

When you anchor Who, What, and Where to real physical things right in front of your child, the guesswork disappears.

Frequently Asked Questions

In what order do children learn Who, What, and Where questions?

Most children ages 2 to 7 master concrete "What" (objects and actions), "Where" (locations), and "Who" (familiar people and pets) questions first, before abstract "Why" or "When" questions.

What should I do if my child repeats the question instead of answering?

Ask the question, pause for 3 seconds, and then immediately model the short answer yourself ("What is Doggy doing? Doggy is eating!") so your child hears the question-and-answer pair without pressure.
