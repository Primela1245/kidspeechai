Why Toddlers Drop the End of Words ("Ba" for Ball, "Ca" for Cat) & 4 Playful Ending-Sound Games

Published 2026-11-24 · Kid Speech AI Early Learning Team · 5 Min Read

✨ Quick Parent Takeaway

If your 15- to 30-month-old says 'Ba' for ball, 'Ca' for cat, 'Do' for dog, or 'Bo' for book, they are using a common early speech pattern called Final Consonant Deletion. To a young toddler learning to coordinate their lips and tongue, an open consonant-vowel ('Ba!') is much easier to produce than closing the mouth a second time at the end of the word! When Is Dropping Ending Sounds Normal? (The 12- to 36-Month Timeline)

When babies and young toddlers (12 to 24 months) first start talking, leaving off the ending consonant (saying "Cu" for cup or "Ha" for hat) is a normal stepping stone—and it counts 100% as a real word! Between 24 and 36 months (ages 2 to 3), toddlers gradually start closing the back gate of the word ("Cu-P!", "Ha-T!", "Bu-S!") so unfamiliar listeners can tell "Cat", "Cap", and "Cab" apart.

4 Playful Games That Help Toddlers Hear & Pop the End of Words

  1. 1. Start With Easy Lip-Popping & Hissing Endings (-P, -B, -M, -T, -S): The easiest final sounds for a toddler to see and copy are -P and -M (where both lips pop shut at the end: "U-P!", "Po-P!", "Cu-P!", "Boo-M!") and -S (a fun snake hiss: "Bu-sss!").
  2. 2. Use the 'Tap Your Lips on the Final Sound' Visual Cue: Never scold or say "No, say cuP." Instead, when your toddler says "U!" to be picked up, smile at eye level, point your index finger to your lips right as you pop the ending -P: "Yes! U-P! Up we go!"

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Do 'Ba' for ball and 'Cu' for cup still count on my toddler's First 100 Words Checklist?

Yes, 100%! A word counts on your toddler's vocabulary checklist based on consistent meaning, not adult pronunciation perfection. Check it off on our Interactive First 100 Words Tracker right away!

Why can my toddler say a 'P' at the beginning of 'Pop' but drops it at the end of 'Cup'?

Putting a consonant at the very end of a word requires a second motor plan after the vowel is already finished. Practicing words that start AND end with the same lip movement ('Pop!', 'Bib', 'Mom', 'Dad', 'Pip') bridges that gap fastest!

When does Final Consonant Deletion typically resolve?

Most toddlers naturally begin adding final consonants between ages 2 and 3 as their vocabulary expands beyond 50 to 100 words.
