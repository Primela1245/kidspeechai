The Mystery of the "One-Hit Wonder" Toddler Word

Almost every parent has experienced this baffling moment: on Tuesday, your toddler points at the kitchen counter and says a crystal-clear "Banana!" or "Puppy!" You are thrilled—and then for the next three weeks, you hold up bananas every morning asking "What's this? Say banana!" and your toddler stays completely silent.

Why does this happen? Because the first time a toddler says a word, it often slips out spontaneously during a moment of zero pressure. When parents suddenly turn that word into a quiz question ("Say it again for Daddy!"), toddlers feel performance pressure and hold back.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did my toddler say a word clearly once and then stop?

Early words start as "fragile" motor patterns in a toddler’s mouth. Spontaneous words come out easily when a child is relaxed, but can temporarily hide if the child feels quizzed or is focusing on another skill.
