Why Toddlers Double Up Syllables ("Baba, Nana, Wawa, Choo-Choo!"): Bridging Baby Words to Clear Speech

Published 2026-11-19 · Kid Speech AI Early Learning Team · 5 Min Read

✨ Quick Parent Takeaway

Between 10 and 20 months, almost every toddler loves 'reduplicated' (doubled-up) two-syllable words like 'Mama, Dada, Baba (bottle), Wawa (water), Nana (banana), Buh-buh (bubbles), and Choo-choo!' Repeating the exact same lip or tongue movement twice in a row is motor-planning gold for a young mouth. Why Doubled-Up Syllables Are the Perfect Stepping Stone

Saying a word with two different consonants and vowels—like "Wa-ter" or "Ba-na-na"—requires a toddler's lips, jaw, and tongue to switch positions mid-word. By doubling one easy syllable ("Wa-wa!" or "Na-na!"), your child masters the rhythm and beat of a multi-syllable word first! Speech-language pathologists celebrate these consistent approximations as real words on your First 100 Words Checklist.

3 Playful Steps to Bridge 'Wawa & Baba' Into Clear Adult Words

  1. 1. Celebrate the Doubled Word, Then Model the 'Two-Beat Clap': When your toddler points at their cup and says "Wawa!", smile and validate them right away while clapping the two real syllables slowly: "Yes, WA-TER! Cold wa-ter!"
  2. 2. Practice 'Change-the-Vowel' Two-Syllable Words First (Ba-by, Pu-ppy, Bu-nny, Coo-kie): Words where the consonant stays similar and only the second vowel changes to "-ee" (like Ba-by, Pu-ppy, Ki-tty, and Tu-mmy) are the easiest bridge from baby words to adult pronunciation!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Is it bad to use words like 'Choo-choo', 'Bye-bye', or 'Night-night' with a late talker?

Not at all! Reduplicated words like 'Bye-bye', 'Night-night', 'Beep-beep', and 'Uh-oh' are much easier for a late talker to imitate than 'Goodbye' or 'Locomotive'. Once they are talking confidently, you naturally pair them ('Choo-choo train!').

At what age do toddlers stop saying 'Wawa' and start saying 'Water'?

Most toddlers begin transitioning from doubled-up approximations ('wawa', 'baba') to two distinct syllables ('wa-ter', 'bo-ttle') between 18 and 26 months as their jaw and tongue coordination matures.

Do 'Wawa' and 'Nana' count on the First 100 Words Checklist?

Yes! As long as your child uses 'Wawa' specifically and consistently to mean water (and not every object in the house), it counts as 1 official spoken word!
