Why Predictable Words Calm Checkup & Haircut Anxiety

When a toddler doesn’t have the words for what is happening to their body, every cold stethoscope or buzzing hair clipper feels like an alarm. Giving your child simple, concrete labels—and practicing those steps on a teddy bear first—puts them back in the driver’s seat so they can say "Tickles!", "My ears!", "Open wide!" or "Break please!"

3 Tear-Free Checkup & Haircut Warm-Up Games

  1. Teddy Bear Checkup With 2-Second Object Naming: Grab a toy doctor kit (or a real flashlight, comb, and spray bottle) and let your child scan their teddy bear’s ears, nose, and shoes with Live Camera Object Naming before giving Teddy a "checkup."

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

When should I start talking to my toddler about an upcoming doctor visit or haircut?

For 2- and 3-year-olds, start playing pretend checkup or barber the day before and the morning of the visit. Reviewing 3 Custom Flashcards right before getting in the car gives them just enough notice without days of worry.

Can we use Kid Speech AI in the waiting room?

Yes! Instead of handing over passive cartoons that cause a meltdown when your name is called, spend 3 minutes scanning shoes, chairs, and books with Live Camera Object Naming or flipping through Pop-Up Flashcards together.

What if my child is non-verbal or a late talker and can't say what hurts?

Practicing pointing to body parts during happy play at home ("Where’s nose? Where’s tummy? Where’s ear?") helps late talkers show you where something feels uncomfortable when they aren't feeling well.
