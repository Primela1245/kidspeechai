✨ Quick Parent Takeaway — First 100 Words Checklist

Why a "Functional" First Words Checklist Beats ABC Memorization

When searching for a 100 first words list or baby first 100 words list, many parents are surprised to learn that pediatric speech-language educators don't start with shapes, colors, or letters. Why? Because a 15-month-old can't use the word "octagon" to ask for a snack or tell you why they are frustrated!

A toddler who can say "milk," "open," "more," "help," and "up" can communicate their wants and feelings 50+ times a day. Combining everyday nouns with early action verbs and social request words is what bridges the gap from single words (12–18 months) to two-word phrases (18–24 months).

3 Playful Daily Routines to Check Off Your Baby's First 100 Words List

  1. The "Clear Plastic Container" Game (Unlocks: Open, Help, More, In, Out): Place a favorite toy car or snack cracker inside a clear snap-lid container your toddler can see through but cannot open alone. Hand it to them, wait 5 seconds for them to look at you, and model: "Open! Help mama open!"
  2. The 3-Object Living Room Camera Hunt (Unlocks 25+ Household Nouns): Open Live Camera Object Naming in Kid Speech AI and let your toddler pick 3 real items in the room—their shoe, their ball, and their cup. Hold the camera steady for 2 seconds so the friendly voice names their exact toy!
  3. The "Ready, Set... GO!" Pause Trick (Unlocks Action Verbs & Exclamations): Roll a ball or push a toy car to the edge of the couch, say "Ready... set..." and pause for 3 full seconds with an excited smile. Even late talkers love filling in the blank with "GO!" or "Whee!"

Frequently Asked Questions: Baby's First 100 Words List

What should be on a baby's first 100 words list for speech and language?

A functional first 100 words list for speech and language balances 6 core categories: People & Family (Mama, Dada, Baby), Social Power Words (More, Please, All done, Help, No, Bye-bye), Food & Drink (Milk, Water, Banana, Cracker), Household & Body Objects (Shoe, Ball, Book, Bed), Action Verbs (Go, Open, Up, Eat, Sleep), and Describing Words (Big, Hot, Wet, Happy).

At what age do toddlers reach 50 to 100 first words?

Most babies say their first 1 to 5 words around 12 months, reach 15 to 20 words by 15–18 months, and hit 50 to 100+ spoken words between 18 and 24 months—which is when two-word combinations like "More milk" and "Open door" typically take off.

Do animal sounds, signs, and word approximations count on a first words checklist?

Yes! Speech-language pathologists count animal sounds ("Moo", "Woof"), exclamatory words ("Uh-oh!", "Whee!"), baby signs, and consistent word approximations ("Ba" for ball or "Wawa" for water) as real words on your baby's first words checklist whenever your child uses them consistently and intentionally.

Should we only practice nouns like "ball" and "apple" on the 100 first words list?

Nouns are great starters, but combining nouns with action verbs ("Roll ball", "Open box") and describing words ("Big truck", "Cold milk") is the secret that unlocks a toddler's first 2- and 3-word phrases!
