What Happens to Toddler Language at 19 and 20 Months?

Nine-teen and twenty months are the crucial "bridge months" right before age two. At this stage, your toddler already understands complex everyday routines (like "Go get your shoes by the door") and is ready to move beyond naming a handful of nouns.

To help a 19- or 20-month-old unlock dozens of new words—and begin experimenting with two-word pairs like "More milk" or "Big ball"—the secret is introducing action verbs and motion.

5 Daily Habits for 19- and 20-Month-Olds

  1. Narrate Live Play Scenes with Your Camera: Open Kid Speech AI and use Real-Time Action Narration during floor play. When your toddler hears complete, friendly descriptions like "The bear is sitting," they naturally absorb how nouns and verbs fit together.
  2. Switch On Pop-Up Video Flashcards: Because verbs are actions in motion, enable Pop-Up Video Flashcards over your child's regular screen time so short real-motion clips pop up and speak clearly on schedule.
  3. Use the "Choice + Action" Question: Ask your 20-month-old, "Should the car roll or stop?" or "Should bear sit or sleep?"
  4. Personalize with Custom Family & Toy Cards: Upload photos and videos of your child's favorite park, pets, and relatives in Custom Photo & Video Flashcards.
  5. Review Your Parent Word Log Together: Check your private on-device Parent Word Log each evening to see which new words and categories your toddler practiced!

Real-Time Action Narration: Point the camera at play scenes to model natural action sentences like "The bear is sitting."

Pop-Up Video Flashcards: Real-motion video clips pop up over screen time so 19- and 20-month-olds connect actions with spoken words.
