Core and Fringe Vocabulary Explained (With Real Toddler Examples)

Why do so many toddler flashcard decks fail to get late talkers speaking? Because they teach 100% Fringe Vocabulary ("Zebra, Octagon, Helicopter, Xylophone")! Think about how often a 2-year-old needs to ask for an octagon (never!) versus how often they need the Core Word "Open" or "Go" (50 times a day—at breakfast, in the playroom, at the door, and in the bath!).

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

What are the best fringe vocabulary examples to teach a 1- or 2-year-old first?

Skip rare zoo animals or shapes and choose 'High-Motivation Personal Fringe Words': your child's Top 5 favorite foods (milk, water, banana, cracker, berry), Top 5 daily objects (shoe, cup, ball, car, book), and family/pet names!

Why is 'Go' considered a core word in speech therapy?

'Go' is a high-frequency core action word because a toddler can use 'Go!' to launch a toy car, ask to be pushed on a swing, ask to go outside, start a chase game, or turn on a wind-up toy.
