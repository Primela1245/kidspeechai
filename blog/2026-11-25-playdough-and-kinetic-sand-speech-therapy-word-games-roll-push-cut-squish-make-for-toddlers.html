Why Fine-Motor Hand Play Supercharges Toddler Action Verbs

Unlike battery-operated toys that do all the work while your child watches passively, playdough does nothing until your toddler acts on it. Every single step—opening the tub, pulling a piece out, rolling a snake, pushing a cookie cutter down, and peeling it up—creates a natural reason to say a high-frequency action verb.

5 Playdough & Sensory Table Speech Routines for Late Talkers

  1. 1. Start With the 'Tight Lid Request' ('Open!', 'Help!', 'Pull!'): Hand your toddler a closed playdough tub and wait 3 seconds. When they hand it back to you, tap the lid near your mouth and say: "Open! ... Open blue!"
  2. 2. Watch the 'Roll the Playdough!' First-Person POV Video Together: Before rolling, play the 15-second Roll the Playdough POV Action Video in Kid Speech AI so your toddler sees eye-level hands rolling dough back and forth while chanting "Roll the playdough!"
  3. 3. Do the '1, 2, 3... SQUISH!' Anticipation Game: Roll a small playdough ball, place your toddler's palm on top, count "1... 2... 3..." and pause until they vocalize or look at you before shouting "SQUISH!" and flattening it!
  4. 4. Hide Small Plastic Animals Inside Dough Balls ('In', 'Out', 'Peekaboo!'): Wrap playdough around a tiny puppy or dinosaur and model "Where's puppy? ... Peel off! ... Take out! ... Hi puppy!"

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Why do speech therapists use playdough in almost every toddler session?

Playdough keeps busy toddlers seated and engaged while creating dozens of natural opportunities to request ('Open', 'More', 'Cut') and imitate action verbs ('Roll', 'Push', 'Squish', 'Poke').

What if my toddler tries to eat playdough?

Use edible homemade oat-or-flour dough or oversized kinetic sensory blocks during 1-on-1 supervised table play, and focus on large motor stamping and squishing words.
