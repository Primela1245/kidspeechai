The 3 Clinical Rules: When 'Uh-Oh!' Counts on Your Baby's First 100 Words Checklist

Pediatric speech-language pathologists use a simple three-part test to decide whether any sound counts as a word: (1) Is it spontaneous? (Your child says it on their own, not just echoing you one second later), (2) Is it consistent? (They use the same sound pattern every time), and (3) Does it carry meaning? (They say "Uh-oh!" when something spills or drops). When your baby drops their sippy cup from the high chair, looks down, and says "Uh-oh!", that is 100% an official spoken word!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Does 'uh-oh' count as a word at the 12-month, 15-month, or 18-month pediatrician visit?

Yes! Pediatricians and speech-language pathologists count 'Uh-oh' as an official word at 12, 15, 18, and 24 months as long as your child uses it meaningfully when something drops, spills, or topples over.

Why does my toddler say 'Uh-oh', 'Whee', and animal sounds, but won't say 'Cup' or 'Shoe'?

Exclamatory words like 'Uh-oh' and 'Whee' rely on simple open vowel melodies and high emotion, whereas nouns like 'Cup' or 'Shoe' require precise tongue and lip coordination. Modeling exclamations and First-Person POV Action Verbs builds the motor confidence needed for everyday nouns!
