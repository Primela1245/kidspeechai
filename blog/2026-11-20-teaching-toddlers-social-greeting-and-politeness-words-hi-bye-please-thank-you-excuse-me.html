Social Greeting & Politeness Words ("Hi, Bye-Bye, Please, Thank You!"): Everyday Wave & Trade Routines

Published 2026-11-20 · Kid Speech AI Early Learning Team · 5 Min Read

✨ Quick Parent Takeaway

Toddlers rarely say 'Hi!' or 'Thank you!' when an adult commands 'Say hi to the nice lady!' at the grocery store—because sudden social pressure makes young kids freeze! Instead, toddlers master social greeting and politeness words ('Hi, Bye-bye, Please, Thank you, Night-night') during playful routines with toys, family photos, and back-and-forth object trades at home. Why 'Say Hi to Grandma!' Makes Toddlers Hide Behind Your Legs

Being told to greet an adult on demand feels like stage fright to a 2-year-old. But watch that exact same toddler when a puppy walks by or when a favorite toy pops out of a box: they wave and shout "Hi doggy!" without anyone asking! By practicing greetings with stuffed animals, household objects, and Custom Family Flashcards first, social words become an automatic habit.

3 Zero-Pressure Games for 'Hi, Bye-Bye, Please & Thank You'

  1. 1. Wave 'Hi!' and 'Bye-Bye!' to Pop-Up & Custom Family Flashcards: Every time a scheduled Pop-Up Flashcard or Custom Family Photo Card (Grandma, Dada, Uncle, Family Dog) appears on screen in Kid Speech AI, wave enthusiastically and say "Hi Grandma!"—and when your child taps to close the card, wave "Bye-bye Grandma!"
  2. 2. The '10-Item Thank-You Trade' Game: Sit on the rug with a bucket of 10 blocks. Hand one block to your toddler and say "Here you go... thank you!" Then hold your hand out so they give it back to you, and say an exaggerated, joyful "THANK YOU!" Hearing "Thank you" modeled 20 times during a fast give-and-take game teaches it 10x faster than withholding snacks until they say 'please'!
  3. 3. Say 'Bye-Bye!' to Inanimate Objects All Day Long: Wave to the bathwater going down the drain ("Bye-bye water!"), the garbage truck driving away ("Bye-bye truck!"), and toys scanned in Live Camera Object Naming when putting them in the bin!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Should I refuse to give my late talker their milk until they say 'Please'?

For a late talker (ages 12–26 months), we want them practicing the specific content word ('Milk!' or 'Open!') rather than using 'Please' as a single cure-all word for everything! Once they can say 'Milk!', model the 2-word expansion: 'Milk please!'

Why is 'Bye-bye' easier for toddlers to say than 'Hello'?

Because 'Bye-bye' uses the easiest early lip-popping consonant ('B') repeated twice, and leaving a room or closing a toy box has a super clear visual ending!

What should I do when a stranger says 'Hi!' and my toddler stays quiet?

Simply model the greeting warmly on your child's behalf without scolding them ('We're waving hi! Hi there!'). Seeing you model it safely builds their confidence to join in next time.
