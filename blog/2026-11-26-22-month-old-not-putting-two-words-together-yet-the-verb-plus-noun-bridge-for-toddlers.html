Why 22 Months Is the Ideal Time to Build the 'Action Verb + Noun' Bridge

At the 24-month pediatric visit, doctors ask two main speech questions: "Does your child say at least 50 words?" and "Are they combining two words together on their own?" Note that memorized chunks like "Thank you", "Bye-bye", or "All done" count as single lexical units. True 2-word combinations join two separate ideas together—like an action verb + an object ("Open door!", "Roll ball!", "Hug bear!").

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Do 'Bye-bye' and 'All done' count as two-word phrases at 22 to 24 months?

Speech therapists count 'Bye-bye', 'All done', and 'Thank you' as single memorized chunks (1 word). A true 2-word phrase combines two independent words that can be remixed, such as 'Open box!', 'More milk!', or 'Bye-bye Dada!'.

How many verbs does a toddler need before putting two words together?

Research shows that toddlers who know at least 10 to 15 action verbs ('Open, Push, Roll, Eat, Go, Help, Wash, Drop') begin combining 2-word phrases weeks earlier than toddlers who only know nouns.
