What Is "Total Conceptual Vocabulary" (And Why Single-Language Checklists Mislead Parents)

Imagine a 24-month-old who says 35 words in Spanish at home with Abuela ("agua, leche, perro, zapatos, más") and 25 different words in English at daycare ("truck, slide, bubble, bye-bye, up"). If a checklist only counts English, it looks like they only have 25 words—below the 50-word milestone! In reality, that child has a healthy 60-word Total Conceptual Vocabulary and is right on track. Even when a toddler mixes both languages in one phrase ("Más milk!" or "Look, perro!"), that code-switching is a sign of smart, resourceful communication.

3 Ways to Boost Bilingual Vocabulary Without Dropping Your Home Language

  1. Keep Your Richest Home Language Strong: Never stop speaking Spanish (or your family’s heritage language) at home! Children build their strongest grammar and sentence foundation when parents speak the language that feels most natural and expressive to them.
  2. Use Custom Photo & Video Flashcards for Home-Language Favorites: Snap photos of family members, favorite home-cooked foods, and bedtime routines in Custom Photo & Video Flashcards so your child hears familiar home-language and English words side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Does speaking two languages at home cause a toddler to have a speech delay?

No! Decades of clinical research prove that growing up bilingual never causes a speech or language disorder. Bilingual toddlers reach their first words and two-word combinations on the exact same timeline when both languages are counted together.

Is it okay if my toddler mixes Spanish and English in the same sentence?

Yes! Mixing two languages in one phrase (called code-switching, like "Quiero more ball") is a completely normal, healthy stage of bilingual development.
