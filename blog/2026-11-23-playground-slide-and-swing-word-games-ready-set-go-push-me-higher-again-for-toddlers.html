Why Movement + Anticipation Is Speech Therapy's #1 Secret Weapon

Sitting still at a table is the hardest place for a high-energy toddler to talk. On a swing or slide, your toddler's sensory system is wide awake, and you control the release of the motion! Every single push of the swing or trip down the slide gives you a built-in, joyful communication turn.

3 Swing & Slide Routines That Spark 20+ Spoken Requests in 10 Minutes

  1. 1. The 'Stand IN FRONT of the Swing' Rule (Face-to-Face 'Ready, Set... GO!'): Never push a toddler swing from behind where your child can't see your face! Stand directly in front of the swing so your toddler is looking right into your eyes. Catch the swing gently after 3 pushes, hold it back, raise your eyebrows, and say: "Ready... Set..." Pause 3 seconds until they look, grin, or shout "GO!" or "Push!"
  2. 2. The Slide Ladder Climb Count ('Up, Up, Up... Sit... WHEE!'): On every step up the slide ladder, chant a rhythmic 1-word action: "Up, up, up! Sit down! 1, 2, 3... WHEE!" Wait at the bottom of the slide with open arms so they can ask "Again!" or "More slide!"

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Why makes standing IN FRONT of the swing so much better than pushing from behind?

When you push from behind, your toddler sees nothing but empty air and doesn't have to communicate with you to keep swinging. When you stand in front of the swing, you share face-to-face eye contact, smiles, and lip-movement cues on every single push!

What if my toddler doesn't say 'Go!' during the 3-second pause on Ready, Set...?

Wait 3 seconds with a big, excited smile—if they kick their legs, make any vowel sound ('Ah!'), or look right at your eyes, shout 'GO!' with enthusiasm and let the swing fly! Within a few trips to the park, they will start filling in 'Go!' on their own.
