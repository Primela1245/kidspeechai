Why Real 3-Angle Photos & First-Person Action Videos Beat 2D Cartoons

Cognitive development research calls it the video transfer deficit: a 1- or 2-year-old who taps a purple cartoon cow on a tablet screen often doesn't connect that cartoon to a real dog, cup, or shoe in your living room. To speak their First 100 Words in real life, toddlers need three things: (1) seeing real objects from multiple angles, (2) watching first-person hand actions that fire mirror neurons for verbs, and (3) naming the exact physical objects in their own home.
