Why "Helper Routines" Teach Location Words Better Than Flashcards Alone

Location words—like in, out, on, under, up, and down—are some of the most useful building blocks in a toddler's vocabulary. Why? Because as soon as your toddler can pair a noun with a location word ("Ball in!", "Bear on!", "Shoes off!"), they have an instant two-word phrase!

And there is no better time to practice location words than when you and your toddler are physically picking up toys and putting them away.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get my toddler to help clean up toys while practicing speech?

Turn cleanup into the "Scan & Dunk" game: let your toddler scan each toy with Kid Speech AI’s Real-Time Camera Object Naming to hear its name spoken out loud right before tossing it "IN!" the basket.

Why are words like "in," "on," and "up" so important for toddlers?

Spatial words (prepositions) act like glue in early speech—allowing a toddler who knows single nouns ("ball", "cup", "bear") to immediately form two-word phrases ("ball in", "cup on", "bear up").
