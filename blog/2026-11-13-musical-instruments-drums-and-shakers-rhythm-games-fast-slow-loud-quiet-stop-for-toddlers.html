How Tapping a Beat Helps Toddlers Say Multi-Syllable Words

When a toddler leaves out the middle syllable of a long word (saying "nana" for "ba-na-na" or "buh-fly" for "but-ter-fly"), their mouth is rushing past the rhythm of the word. Giving your child’s hands a physical beat to tap on an upside-down kitchen bowl or oatmeal drum slows their motor planning down so each syllable gets its own clear tap: "Ba (tap) - na (tap) - na (tap)!"

3 Kitchen Drum & Shaker Rhythm Word Games

  1. The "Fast, Slow... FREEZE!" Shaker Game: Fill a clean plastic water bottle with dry rice or pasta (screw the cap on tightly!) to make a homemade maraca. Shake it together while chanting "Shake, shake, shake... STOP!" Then contrast opposites: "Shake fast-fast-fast... now shake sloooow!"
  2. Drum Out the Syllables of Scanned Objects: Turn an empty oatmeal container or plastic mixing bowl upside down as a drum. Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Do I need to buy special toy instruments for rhythm speech games?

Not at all! An upside-down plastic mixing bowl, two wooden spoons, an empty cardboard oatmeal tub, or a sealed Tupperware cup with 10 dry Cheerios inside make the best toddler drums and shakers.

Why does "Stop and Go" music play help toddlers who don't seem to listen?

Because freezing during a drum game makes listening a physical game! Your child eagerly watches your face and listens for the word "GO!" so they can bang the drum again.

How does clapping or drumming syllables help 4- to 7-year-olds get ready to read?

Breaking spoken words into syllable beats (phonological segmentation) is one of the foundational pre-reading skills taught in kindergarten and first grade!
