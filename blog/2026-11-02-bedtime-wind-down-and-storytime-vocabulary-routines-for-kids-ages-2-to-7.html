Why Pre-Sleep Word Review Sticks So Deeply

Early-childhood memory research consistently shows that children consolidate newly heard words during slow-wave sleep. When the last thing your child hears before tucking in is a calm, familiar review of real objects, family faces, and gentle two-word phrases, those words are far more likely to pop out spontaneously the next morning.

The 5-Minute "Goodnight Words" Routine

  1. Step 1: The "Goodnight Bedroom" 2-Second Walk (2 Minutes): Dim the overhead lights and walk slowly to 4 bedroom items: Pillow, Blanket, Teddy Bear, and Book. Hold a calm 2-second focus on each item so your child hears its name spoken clearly, then wave and say, "Night-night, pillow!"
  2. Step 2: Today's 1 Word × 3 Studio Angles (1 Minute): Open today's daily Montessori studio word (Front View, Side Profile, and 3/4 Back POV) on clean white backgrounds—zero flashing animations, just one real-world object from three perspectives.
  3. Step 3: Family "Night-Night" Cards (2 Minutes): Flip through 3 to 4 Custom Family Photo Cards (Grandma, Grandpa, Auntie, family dog) so your child can say "Night-night, Grandma!" before lights out.
