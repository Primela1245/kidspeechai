Why Pretend Play & Expressive Language Grow Hand-in-Hand

In speech-language pathology, pretending that an empty plastic spoon has imaginary soup on it (symbolic play) uses the exact same cognitive skill as understanding that the spoken sound "Bear" represents a fuzzy toy (symbolic language). Toddlers who act out simple 2-step caregiving routines with a teddy bear transition into 2- and 3-word sentences significantly faster.

5 Step-by-Step Teddy Bear & Stuffed Animal Speech Games for Toddlers

  1. 1. Co-View the 'Hug the Bear!' & 'Feed the Bear!' First-Person POV Action Videos: Open Kid Speech AI so your toddler sees eye-level toddler hands wrapping a fluffy brown teddy bear in a warm hug and feeding it with a spoon while hearing "Hug the bear!" and "Feed the bear!"
  2. 2. Host a 3-Minute Teddy Bear Picnic ('Bear Hungry!' → 'Feed Bear!'): Sit your child's teddy bear next to a toy bowl and spoon. Rub your tummy and say: "Oh, bear is hungry! Let's feed the bear! ... Eat, bear! ... Mmm, yummy!"

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

At what age do toddlers start pretend play like feeding a teddy bear?

Early pretend play typically begins around 15 to 18 months (hugging a bear, holding a toy cup to its mouth) and expands into 2- and 3-step pretend sequences (feeding the bear, wiping its mouth, and putting it to bed) between 19 and 24 months.

What if my toddler prefers trucks or dinosaurs over teddy bears?

Use a plastic dinosaur or dump truck as the character! Toddlers think it is hilarious to 'Feed the dinosaur!' with a toy spoon, 'Wash the truck!', or put a dinosaur to bed ('Night-night dino!').
