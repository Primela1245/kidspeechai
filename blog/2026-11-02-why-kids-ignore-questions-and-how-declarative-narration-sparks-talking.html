The Hidden Trap of "Say Apple! What's That?"

When parents worry about a child's vocabulary, it is completely natural to ask lots of test questions: "What color is that? Say doggy! Can you say cup?"

Unfortunately, even toddlers as young as 18 to 24 months recognize when a question is a test. Instead of feeling like a shared game, constant quizzing puts the spotlight on what the child can't easily say yet—causing them to look away, grunt, or throw the toy.

How Declarative Narration Unlocks Spontaneous Speech

  1. Follow Their Eyes (The 2-Second Focus Rule): Instead of making your child look at what you are holding, let them look at what excites them. When Kid Speech AI detects that the camera has stopped moving for 2 seconds, it simply announces the object out loud—zero commands required.
  2. Use the 3-to-1 Comment Rule: For every 1 question you ask, offer 3 warm declarative comments: "Fire truck! Big red truck! Going fast!"
  3. Let 27 Voices Model the Word for You: Sometimes kids resist repeating a parent who asks all day, yet gleefully copy one of Kid Speech AI's 27 cheerful voices when it names their toy car!

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my toddler clam up when I say "Say ball!" or "What color is this?"

Direct commands and constant test questions feel like a performance test. Switching to declarative narration—simply naming what your child is looking at without demanding a response—removes pressure and invites spontaneous imitation.

What is the 3-to-1 Rule for parent comments versus questions?

Aim to make at least 3 simple declarative statements ("Ball! Big blue ball! Rolling fast!") for every 1 question you ask during play.
