Why Toddlers Say "You Want Cookie" Instead of "I Want Cookie"

A ball is always called a "ball" no matter who holds it. Pronouns break that rule: when you point to your child and say "Do you want a snack?", your child hears themselves called "you". So when they ask for a snack, they dutifully repeat, "You want a snack!"

The secret to fixing pronoun mix-ups is modeling first-person phrases ("I", "My", "Mine") from your child's point of view.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do toddlers mix up "You" and "I" or refer to themselves by their first name?

Unlike concrete nouns ("cup" is always a cup), pronouns shift depending on who is talking. When parents ask "Do you want milk?", children naturally echo "You want milk" until they hear "I" and "My" modeled from their own perspective.

Which pronouns should we practice first at home?

Start with ownership and self-reference words ("My", "Mine", "Me", "I") paired with your child's real shoes, cup, and toys before contrasting with "Your" and "You".
